# Exponential ergodicity and the space-time Gibbs states of one-dimensional probabilistic cellular automata

Seth Douglas and Nidhal Mghirbi. Version 1.2.0, November 2026. DOI: [10.5281/zenodo.24122307](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24122307). Version 1.1.0: [10.5281/zenodo.24023364](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24023364). Concept DOI (all versions): [10.5281/zenodo.24023363](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24023363).

## Abstract

A positive-rate probabilistic cellular automaton (PCA) defines a Gibbs specification in one more dimension, its space–time interaction, in which the future is a boundary like the past and the sides. For finite-alphabet, finite-radius, strictly positive PCA on $\mathbb{Z}$ we prove that uniform exponential ergodicity of the dynamics is equivalent to exponential weak spatial mixing of this specification in arbitrary finite volumes of $\mathbb{Z}^2$ under arbitrary exterior conditions, so that the specification has exactly one DLR state, the stationary trajectory law, with exponentially decaying correlations. This answers in one dimension, under exponential ergodicity, the question of Lebowitz, Maes and Speer whether every Gibbs measure of the space–time interaction is a trajectory law of the PCA, previously known for periodic measures. The forward proof removes the far future boundary from Bérard's coupling from the past at an entropy cost that only one-dimensional geometry absorbs. In every dimension, cutting the cluster of coupling failures at a late level, where its accumulated size dominates its width, removes the future boundary whenever the dynamics has a coalescing local flow satisfying the quantitative small-badness hypothesis of the prefix-cap theorem. In particular, if some basic i.i.d. random-map representation coalesces exponentially for all inputs at once, the specification has exactly one DLR state and exponential weak mixing in arbitrary volumes, and for attractive binary PCA in every dimension uniform exponential ergodicity is equivalent to exponential weak mixing. In every dimension and with no rate, the relative-entropy defect of any Gibbs measure from the dynamics is a surface term; hence for an ergodic PCA the stationary trajectory is the only uniformly recurrent Gibbs measure, the translation action on the Gibbs simplex is uniquely ergodic, and all-DLR uniqueness holds exactly when every Gibbs measure is uniformly recurrent. Finite certificates for the hypothesis are exactly computable; for the noisy majority rule they give a unique space–time Gibbs state down to noise 0.485, including noise one half, and Marsan, Sablik and Törmä's fixed radius-one automaton has one space–time Gibbs state at small noise (granting structural estimates from their low-noise proof) and at large noise, and several in between. Finally, as an explicit witness of a classical phenomenon, the synchronous heat-bath dynamics of the anisotropic Ising model at critical couplings is a positive PCA on $\mathbb{Z}^2$ with a rational table that is uniformly ergodic but not exponentially ergodic: granting two classical exact formulas for the Ising model, the largest distance between the laws of one site at time $t$ from two initial rows is at least $(t+1)^{-1/8}/4$.

## What the paper proves

A probabilistic cellular automaton (PCA) updates every cell of a row at once. Each cell picks its next state at random, from one fixed table that reads a few neighbouring cells of the previous row. Recording a run row by row gives a random picture with one more dimension, time. The transition probabilities also define an equilibrium system on that picture, the space-time Gibbs specification, which treats the future rows as a boundary condition exactly like the past rows and the sides. The paper asks when this equilibrium system has no states other than the one the dynamics produces. Throughout, the alphabet and the radius are finite and every table entry is positive.

- **Theorem A (Theorem 3.1), one dimension.** A PCA on the line forgets its initial row exponentially fast, uniformly (uniform exponential ergodicity), exactly when its space-time specification mixes exponentially in every finite volume of the plane, whatever is fixed outside the volume, the future included. The specification then has exactly one Gibbs (DLR) state, the law of the stationary run, and its correlations decay exponentially.
- **Theorem B (Proposition 9.1, Theorems 9.2, 10.3 and 10.10, Corollary 10.12), every dimension.** Exponential mixing of the specification implies uniform exponential ergodicity, and a unique Gibbs state alone implies uniform local convergence of the dynamics, without a rate. For every Gibbs state, the relative-entropy defect from the forward dynamics in a finite volume is at most log(1/p₋) times the number of exterior children of the volume, where p₋ is the least table entry: a surface term. Hence, for an ergodic PCA, the stationary run is the only uniformly recurrent Gibbs state, the translation action on the Gibbs states is uniquely ergodic, and the Gibbs state is unique exactly when every Gibbs state is uniformly recurrent.
- **Theorem C (Propositions 4.1 to 4.4).** Finite certificates, polynomial inequalities that are exactly computable in rational arithmetic, imply the hypothesis of Theorem A.
- **Theorem D (Section 11), examples on the line.** Every radius-r rule with uniform noise above 1 − 1/(2r+1), and the noisy shift at every positive noise, have a unique space-time Gibbs state. For the noisy majority rule, depth-seven certificates give a unique space-time Gibbs state at noise 1/2 and at noise 0.485 (Table 2); above noise 1/2 the same conclusion follows from the high-noise bound of Lebowitz, Maes and Speer combined with Theorems A and C.
- **Theorem E (Section 11.5).** The fixed radius-one automaton of Marsan, Sablik and Törmä, perturbed by uniform noise, has one space-time Gibbs state at small noise (granting structural estimates from their low-noise proof, which the paper does not re-derive) and at large noise, and several at some noise in between.
- **Theorem F (Theorem 13.3, Corollaries 13.7 and 13.8), every dimension, under coalescence.** If the dynamics has a coalescing local flow with the properties listed in Definition 13.1 whose badness probability satisfies the smallness condition (51) of Theorem 13.3, then the space-time specification has exactly one Gibbs (DLR) state, the law of the stationary run; no invariance is assumed. The proof charges the future boundary at a late level of the cluster of coupling failures, where its accumulated size dominates its width, instead of at the last level, which is what limits Theorem A to one dimension. If some basic random-map representation of the table coalesces exponentially for all initial rows at once, the specification also mixes exponentially in every finite volume. For attractive PCA with two symbols, this happens exactly when the PCA is uniformly exponentially ergodic, so the equivalence of Theorem A holds for them in every dimension. Whether every uniformly exponentially ergodic PCA in two or more dimensions has such a flow is open; constructing one is a sufficient route to the all-DLR uniqueness part of Open problem 14.1.
- **Theorem G (Theorem 11.8), a critical example.** The synchronous heat-bath dynamics of the anisotropic Ising model on the square lattice at the critical couplings e^{2J_h} = 3, e^{2J_v} = 2 is a positive PCA on the plane with nine rational table entries. Granting two classical exact formulas for the Ising model (the spontaneous magnetization and the diagonal Toeplitz correlations, both in the low-temperature phase), it is uniformly ergodic, but the largest distance between the laws of one site at time t from two initial rows is at least (1/4)(t+1)^(-1/8), so it is not exponentially ergodic. This is an explicit witness of a classical phenomenon, slow relaxation at a critical point; it answers in two dimensions the question whether an intermediate rate can occur at all (Open problem 14.2), which stays open on the line.

In one dimension the paper also shows that the Dobrushin-Shlosman block conditions for the space-time specification and the past-only conditions of Maes and Shlosman hold for the same positive tables (Proposition 12.2, Corollary 12.3). Together these results partially answer Problems 2 and 4 of Lebowitz, Maes and Speer (1990).

The proofs build on prior work that the paper's "Credit" section attributes in detail: the translation-invariant correspondence of Goldstein, Kuik, Lebowitz and Maes; Bérard's coupling from the past for exponentially ergodic one-dimensional PCA; the constructive ergodicity criteria of Maes and Shlosman; Halawi's proof that ergodic positive PCA are uniformly ergodic; and, on the equilibrium side, the theorems of Dobrushin and Shlosman and of Martinelli, Olivieri and Schonmann. Section 13 credits the translation-invariant and high-noise results it does not claim (Goldstein, Kuik, Lebowitz and Maes; van den Berg and Steif; Dobrushin and Shlosman; van den Berg and Maes), and Section 11.6 the classical Ising formulas of Onsager, Yang, Chang, Montroll, Potts and Ward, and McCoy and Wu.

Start with `verification/VERIFY.md`. `cd verification/code && sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS` checks the recorded files in seconds; `cd verification/independent && python -B table2_check.py` re-derives the table in seconds; recomputing it with the first program takes 12 to 20 minutes.
- `verification/v1.2/`: the checks added in version 1.2.0, each a few seconds except the one-minute Monte Carlo test: `check_cap_identity_and_messages.py` (the cap identity with all exterior-child factors and the message bound of Lemma 13.4, exactly, on small examples in one and two dimensions), `check_algebra.py` (the table, Cauchy's determinant, the Toeplitz limits and the constants of Lemma 11.7) and `check_critical_mc.py` (a registered Monte Carlo refutation attempt on the bound of Theorem 11.8, with a control), with their recorded outputs and resource receipts. Start with `verification/v1.2/VERIFY.md`.
